# How to Play Clout

You are running for election. Win **more than half of all votes**.

The catch: you can't see what the voters want. You have to **find out**, **promise a plan** they like, then
**campaign** so the message spreads before election day.

A game takes about 10 minutes.

---

## The game at a glance

| Stage | What you do | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Set up** | Choose the size of the country, the number of rounds and your starting money | Setup screen |
| **2. Research** | Ask survey questions to learn what each region wants | Survey screen, **Research** tab |
| **3. Manifesto** | Split a government budget across five sectors, then lock it in | Survey screen, **Manifesto** tab |
| **4. Campaign** | Each round, hire party workers and buy local media | Campaign screen |
| **5. Election** | Votes are counted and the hidden truth is revealed | Results screen |

All spending comes from **one treasury** (shown as **Đ**). Money spent on research is money you can't spend on the
campaign, and money left at the end is worth nothing.

---

## 1. Set up a game

| Setting | What it means | First game |
|---|---|---|
| **Voters** | How many people live in the country (1,000 to 1,000,000) | 10,000 |
| **World seed** | Picks the world. The same seed always builds the same country, so you can replay it or share it. | any |
| **Campaign rounds** | How many rounds you get to campaign (3 to 5) | 5 |
| **Starting money** | Your treasury, from Đ500 to Đ1,000,000 | Đ500 |

Press **Start campaign**.

---

## 2. Research: find out what voters want

The country has four regions: **North, South, East and West**. Each region has a hidden **archetype**, a typical
idea of how the budget should be spent. For example, an *Agrarian heartland* wants lots of agriculture; an
*Industrial belt* wants industry. No two regions share an archetype.

**What you get for free:**
- the size of each region;
- the **national top 3**: which sectors most people rank in their top three (it doesn't say where they live);
- a **poll** of 400 voters each round, with its margin of error.

**What you can buy, at Đ10 per question:**
> "In **North**, what share of voters want more **Agriculture** than **Education**?"

- Each answer comes from a fresh sample of 10% of the region, so it has a **margin of error**.
- Asking the same question again in the same round **combines the samples** and narrows the margin.

**Region dossiers** rank which archetypes fit your answers so far. When you're fairly sure about a region, set a
**hunch** for it. Hunches feed the manifesto tools in the next step.

> 💡 Two or three well-chosen questions are usually enough. Ask about two sectors that would split the archetypes you
> are unsure about.

---

## 3. Manifesto: promise a budget

Your **manifesto** splits the budget across five sectors: **Education, Welfare, Agriculture, Industry and
Environment**. The shares must add up to **100%**.

- **Blend my hunches** gives you a quick start. Pick 2 to 4 regions and it mixes their archetypes, giving more weight
  to bigger regions.
- The **Match with each archetype** meter shows how a typical voter of each archetype would feel. Green means they'd
  lean your way.
- When you're happy, press **Commit manifesto** and confirm. **You can't change it afterwards.**

**How voters judge you.** A voter backs you only when your plan is **close** to their own: it must match about
**82.5%** of their ideal split. A plan that tries to please everyone usually ends up close to no one.

> 💡 Aim at **2 or 3 regions that want similar things** and together hold more than half the voters.

---

## 4. Campaign: win voters over

Each round, choose what to buy, then press **Launch round**. In the last round the button reads
**Final push — go to the polls**.

| Tool | Cost | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Party worker** | Đ20 each, paid once | A real voter in that region joins your party for good. In the round you hire them, they try to convert their friends. Each convert then tries *their* friends the next round, so the message spreads **one step per round**. |
| **Local media** | about Đ50 per airing (cheaper in smaller regions) | Reaches about 60% of a region at once and pulls them towards your plan. The effect is temporary, and each repeat airing in the same region is weaker. At most once per region per round. |
| **Survey question** | Đ10 | You can keep asking questions during the campaign. |

**Things to know:**
- **Hire workers early.** A worker hired in round 1 gets five rounds of spreading; one hired in the last round gets one.
- **Workers convert people who already half-agree with you** much more easily. A good manifesto makes workers stronger.
- **Each voter can be approached at most 3 times** in a game, so piling workers into one place soon stops paying off.
- **Opinions keep moving on their own.** Between rounds, every voter drifts a little towards what their friends think.
- **A few voters never change their mind** (zealots), but their friends still listen to them.

**Following your progress:**
- The **poll** at the start of each round shows your estimated vote share and its margin of error.
- Click a past round to **look back** at what it bought and what changed.
- Turn on the **round clock** for time pressure. When it runs out, the round is skipped with nothing bought.

---

## 5. Election and results

After the last round the votes are counted. You win with **more than half** of them; a tie is a loss.

The results screen then shows:
- whether you won, and by how much;
- what each region **really** wanted and how it could be moved, and whether your hunches were right;
- how your manifesto compares with the best possible one (the **Oracle**, which saw everything);
- how many points your campaign added compared with doing nothing;
- how your message spread through the friendship network;
- a **round-by-round debrief** of what you knew at each point: whether a win was still possible, and whether it was
  guaranteed.

---

## Tips for your first game

1. **Don't overspend on research.** Two or three questions, then keep the rest for the campaign.
2. **Pick a winning coalition, not the whole country.** Two or three regions that agree beat a plan for everyone.
3. **Hire a few workers in round 1.** Early workers spread the furthest, and a handful is usually enough.
4. **Spread media across regions and rounds.** Repeat airings in one region lose strength.
5. **Watch the polls.** If a region isn't moving, spend somewhere else.
6. **Spend everything.** Leftover money doesn't count.

---

## Words used in the game

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Archetype** | A region's typical budget preference, such as *Agrarian heartland* or *Green urban*. Hidden. |
| **Personality** | How a region's voters are moved: by friends, by media or by party workers. Hidden. |
| **Manifesto** | Your promised budget split. Fixed once committed. |
| **Hunch** | Your guess at a region's archetype. Used by *Blend my hunches*. |
| **Worker** | A voter you hire. They back your manifesto for good and try to convert their friends. |
| **Convert** | A voter won over by a worker or by another convert. |
| **Zealot** | A voter who never changes their mind. |
| **Poll** | A free sample of 400 voters each round, estimating your vote share. |
| **Oracle** | A computer player that sees everything. Used only as a benchmark after the game. |
