# How to Play Clout

## The goal

You are running for election. There are four regions, and their voters vote after the last campaign round.
You win if you get **more than half of all votes**.

The catch: you don't know what the voters want. You have to find out, pick a plan they like, and then
campaign to spread it.

## The big picture

The game has three stages:

1. **Research and manifesto.** Find out what each region wants, then commit to a budget plan.
2. **Campaign rounds.** Spend money each round to win voters over.
3. **Election.** Votes are counted and you see how you did.

Everything costs money from one treasury, so spending more on research leaves less for the campaign.

## 1. Start a game

Choose how many voters there are, how many campaign rounds you get, and how much money you start with.
The seed is optional. The same seed gives you the same world again.

## 2. Find out what voters want

Your manifesto splits a budget across five sectors: **Education, Welfare, Agriculture, Industry and
Environment**. Each voter has their own ideal split.

- The **national top 3** is free. It shows which sectors people care about most across the country, but not
  how the regions differ.
- Each region follows one **archetype** (a typical budget profile). No two regions share one. Your job is to
  work out which archetype lives where.
- You learn this by **paying for survey questions**, such as "What share of North voters want more Industry
  than Education?" Each answer has a margin of error. Asking the same question again narrows it.
- The **region dossiers** rank which archetypes best fit your answers so far. When you are fairly sure, set a
  **hunch** for that region.
- Each region also has a **personality**: how easily its voters are swayed by friends, by media, or by party
  workers. This matters later.

## 3. Write your manifesto

Set the five sliders so the shares add up to 100%. A quick way to start is **Blend my hunches**. It mixes the
archetypes you picked for chosen regions, giving more weight to regions with more voters.

Voters only back you if your plan is **close to what they want**. A plan that tries to please everyone can end
up close to nobody. It is often better to aim at **2 or 3 regions** that together hold more than half the
voters and want similar things.

The match meter shows how each archetype would feel about your plan. Green means a typical voter would lean
your way. When you're happy, **lock in** the manifesto. You can't change it after that.

## 4. Campaign

Each round you decide how to spend money, then launch the round. Your tools:

- **Party workers.** You pay a one-time fee to recruit a worker in a region. The worker is a real voter who now
  fully backs your manifesto. In the round you buy them, they try to convert their friends. Friends they win
  over then try their own friends the next round, so the message spreads one step per round. Buy workers
  early to let the spread go further. Workers convert people who already half-agree with you much more easily.
- **Local media.** Run ads in a region to pull its voters toward your plan. Running ads in the same region
  again has less effect each time.
- **More survey questions.** You can still ask questions to check how things are going.

Voters also influence each other through their friends between rounds, so opinions keep shifting on their own.

At the start of each round you get a **poll** showing your estimated vote share and its margin of error. Use it
to see whether you are gaining ground. You can click a past round to look back at what happened. You can also
turn on a round timer if you want time pressure.

## 5. Election

After the last round, the votes are counted. The results screen shows:

- whether you won, and by how much,
- what each region really wanted,
- how your manifesto compares with the best possible one (the "oracle"),
- how many points your campaign added compared with doing nothing,
- how your message spread through the friend network.

## Tips

- Don't spend all your money on research. You need some for the campaign.
- Ask about sector pairs that separate the archetypes you are unsure about.
- Pick a winning group of regions, not the whole country.
- Spend on workers early and on media where voters respond to media.
- Watch the polls. If one region isn't moving, spend somewhere else.
