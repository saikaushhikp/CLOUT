# R4: Does the best manifesto stay the best as opinions drift?

## Question

With no campaign, voters slowly move towards the average opinion of their friends. So the manifesto that wins the
most votes today may not be the one that wins the most votes in a few rounds. R4 asks:

- How fast does the best manifesto move as voters drift?
- How many votes do you lose if you pick the manifesto for one round and the vote happens in a different round?
- The game lasts 3 to 5 rounds. Which round should the manifesto be designed for?

## Setup

- **Worlds.** Seeds 12000–12099 (100 seeds). These are the R1 worlds, so b\*0 here is R1's round-0 optimum.
  It matches R1's E\*0 on every seed to 6·10⁻⁸ (`checks.r1_consistency`). N = 10,000, default config.
- **Two paired populations**, built as in R2 (`r2_drift_no_campaign.world_pair`):
  - *zealots* (primary): the default 5% zealots, who never change their mind;
  - *normal* (secondary): no zealots.
  - v(0), the friendship graph, the regions and every non-zealot's susceptibility are identical in the two
    (`checks.identity_check_all` = true).
- **Drift.** No campaign. Each round every voter moves a small step towards the average of their friends:
  v_i ← (1 − a_i) v_i + a_i · mean(friends), with a_i = 0.15·α_i. This is the game's own update
  (`r2.social_run`, the same code as `oracle.anticipate`). Our round-5 electorate equals `oracle.anticipate` to
  1.2·10⁻⁷ (`checks.v5_vs_oracle_anticipate_max_abs`). We record rounds 0–10, 20 and 50.
- **Best manifesto per round, b\*_s.** For every recorded round s we search for the manifesto with the highest
  expected vote share on that round's voters. The search is R1's batched multi-start projected gradient (step 0.02,
  ≤ 400 iterations). It uses R1's 27 starting points computed on v(s), plus a warm start at the previous round's
  optimum (28 starts). We keep the best exact score over every iterate.
- **Scoring.** Every b\*_s, and the library election-day manifesto `oracle_manifesto` (optimised on the 5-round
  forecast), is scored at every recorded round t. This gives a matrix E[s, t].
  - E\*(t) = E[t, t] is the best possible vote share at round t.
  - **Regret** regret[s, t] = E\*(t) − E[s, t] is the vote share lost by keeping the round-s manifesto when the vote
    is at round t.
- **Worlds split.** "Divided": the archetypes of the two largest regions differ by TV ≥ 0.35 (52 seeds). "United":
  the rest (48 seeds). This is R1's split; R1 calls the second group "aligned".
- **Statistics.** Means with 95% t-CIs. Rates with Wilson CIs. Paired Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction
  (`summary.<pop>.tests`). Divided vs united uses Mann–Whitney with Holm (`scenario_split.between`).
- **Compute.** cuda:2 (NVIDIA RTX A5000, 24 GB). 634 s wall time in total: 180 s to build the worlds and ORACLE manifestos,
  409 s for drift, optimisation and scoring. Batches of 50 seeds × 2 populations; the optimiser works on
  [50 seeds, 28 starts, 10,000 voters, 5] tensors. Peak GPU memory was 2.66 GiB and mean GPU use was 58%
  (`provenance.compute`).
- **CPU↔GPU parity** on seeds 12000–12002 (zealot population, design rounds 0, 3, 5): passed. All 7 metrics agree
  exactly (`provenance.parity`).
- **Deviations.**
  - We optimised at *every* recorded round (0–10, 20, 50). This is a superset of the requested design rounds
    {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 20, 50}.
  - We call R1's "aligned" worlds "united".
- **Optimiser check.** A negative regret would mean the optimiser missed a better manifesto. No cell of the
  13 × 13 × 100 regret array was below −10⁻⁴ in either population; the minimum was 0
  (`summary.zealots.negative_regret`).

Unless stated otherwise, the numbers are for the zealot population. A vote share difference of 0.01 means about
**100 voters out of 10,000**.

### Key decision outputs (`summary.zealots.decision`, `summary.zealots.tests`)

All regrets below are > 0 on 100/100 seeds, Holm p = 5.8·10⁻¹⁷.

**(i) Designing for round 0 and voting later.**
- Vote at round 3: regret 0.0037 [0.0027, 0.0046], about 37 votes (`decision.regret_design0_vote3`).
- Vote at round 5: regret 0.0099 [0.0078, 0.0120], about 99 votes (`decision.regret_design0_vote5`).
- Later the loss grows fast: 0.033 at round 10 (333 votes) and 0.128 at round 50 (`regret.0.10`, `regret.0.50`).

**(ii) Is "design for the final round" robust across game lengths 3–5?**
- Design for 5, vote at 3: regret 0.0014 [0.0010, 0.0017], 14 votes (`decision.regret_design5_vote3`).
- Design for 3, vote at 5: regret 0.0015 [0.0011, 0.0018], 15 votes (`decision.regret_design3_vote5`).
- Design for 5 instead of 0 when voting at round 3 saves 0.0023 [0.0014, 0.0031]. It is better on 96/100 seeds
  (`tests["regret[0,3]-regret[5,3]"]`, Holm p = 3.3·10⁻¹⁶).
- **Worst case over game lengths 3, 4, 5** (`decision.worst_case_regret_over_T3to5`):

  | design for round | 0 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | worst-case regret | 0.0099 | 0.0015 | **0.0004** | 0.0014 |
  | about votes | 99 | 15 | **4** | 14 |

  Designing for the **middle round 4** is best: `decision.best_design_by_mean_worst_case` = 4. Its worst case is
  0.0004 [0.0003, 0.0005], 0.0010 below designing for round 5 (`tests["worst3to5[design5]-worst3to5[design4]"]`,
  100/100 seeds, Holm p = 5.8·10⁻¹⁷).

**(iii) How many rounds until the optimum moves TV ≥ 0.05?** (`tv_to_bstar0`, `first_round_tv_ge_0.05`)
- The *mean* distance TV(b\*_t, b\*0) first reaches 0.05 at round 6 (`mean_tv_first_round_ge_0.05`). It is 0.032
  [0.023, 0.042] at round 3 and 0.047 [0.038, 0.057] at round 5.
- Per seed the picture is uneven. The median seed reaches 0.05 only at round 10. 31/100 seeds [23%, 41%] have moved
  ≥ 0.05 by round 5 (`rate_tv_ge_0.05.5`), and 28 seeds never move that far, even by round 50.
- The move flattens out: TV is 0.069 at round 10 and 0.089 at round 50.

### Crossover: when does the election-day manifesto overtake the round-0 one? (`crossover_b5_over_b0`)

The crossover round is the first round where b\*5 gets more expected votes than b\*0.

| Round | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | never by 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| seeds | 3 | 11 | 82 | 4 | 0 |

The median is round 3. 96/100 seeds [90%, 98%] have crossed by round 3, and all 100 [96%, 100%] by round 5. This
agrees with R2.

### Where the optimum's votes come from (`support_by_rank`, `carried_count`, `sector_abs_change_from_b0`)

- **Support by region.** Under b\*_t the largest region (R1) gives a 0.57 mean vote probability at round 0 and 0.59
  at round 5. The three smaller regions rise much faster: R2 from 0.32 to 0.44, R4 from 0.22 to 0.35.
- **Regions carried.** A region is carried when its mean vote probability is above 0.5. The optimum carries 1.31
  regions at round 0, 1.79 at round 5 and 2.43 at round 10 (`carried_count`). The share of voters in carried regions
  goes 0.39 → 0.50 → 0.65 (`carried_share`).
- **Sectors.** The average optimum barely changes sector by sector. The mean absolute change from b\*0 is
  0.014–0.023 per sector at round 5. The largest changes are in Agri and Industry, and Env changes least
  (`sector_abs_change_from_b0.5`).
- **Showcase seed 12000** (divided, Figure e). The optimum does not slide smoothly. Between rounds 2 and 3 it jumps
  from an Agri-heavy platform (Agri 0.29) to an Industry/Edu one (Industry 0.24, Agri 0.20), then stays nearly fixed.
  The ORACLE manifesto sits on top of b\*5.

### ORACLE consistency check

- TV(ORACLE, b\*5) = 0.0036 [0.0015, 0.0058] (`tv_oracle_to_bstar5`). The library's election-day manifesto is
  essentially b\*5.
- At round 5, E(ORACLE) − E\*(5) = −0.000045 [−0.000075, −0.000015], under 1 vote per 10,000
  (`tests["E_oracle(5)-E*(5)"]`). The 28-start search finds a slightly better manifesto on every seed, but the
  difference is negligible.

### No-zealot population (secondary; `summary.normal`, `summary.zealots_minus_normal`)

The results are almost the same.
- Regret[0, 5] = 0.0109 [0.0086, 0.0131], regret[5, 3] = 0.0015, and the best design round is again 4 (worst case
  0.0005).
- The median crossover is again round 3 (97/100 seeds by round 3).
- Zealots slightly *reduce* the cost of a stale manifesto because the electorate drifts less. For example,
  regret[0, 5] is 0.0010 lower with zealots (Holm p = 2.7·10⁻¹⁷).
- Zealots also lower the best possible share: E\*(5) is 0.0057 lower and E\*(50) is 0.035 lower
  (`zealots_minus_normal`).

## What it shows

Terms used below:
- A **manifesto** is how a party splits its promises over 5 sectors.
- A voter's **agreement** is how close the manifesto is to their own split.
- **Expected vote share** is the average chance that a voter votes for you. 0.01 is about 100 of the 10,000 voters.
- **Regret** is the votes you lose by using a manifesto built for a different round than the vote, compared with
  the best manifesto for the voting round.
- **TV distance** measures how different two manifestos are: 0 means identical, 1 means no overlap. TV = 0.05 means
  5% of the promise budget sits in different sectors.

1. **The best manifesto does move, but slowly.**
   - The average optimum shifts by 0.03 TV in 3 rounds and 0.05 TV by round 6. After about round 10 it moves very
     little.
   - In many worlds it hardly moves at all. In some, like seed 12000, it jumps suddenly from one kind of platform to
     another when the drifting voters make a different compromise the better choice.
2. **Keeping today's best manifesto costs a little in a short game, a lot in a long one.**
   - If the vote is at round 3, the round-0 manifesto loses about 37 votes per 10,000. At round 5 it loses about 99.
     This happens on every one of the 100 worlds.
   - At round 10 it loses about 330, and at round 50 about 1,280.
   - The reason: drift pulls the smaller regions towards the middle. The best manifesto follows them, and the
     round-0 one, built mainly around the largest region, does not.
3. **The election-day manifesto overtakes the round-0 one around round 3.** This was true on 82 of 100 worlds, and
   on all 100 by round 5.
4. **If the game length is 3, 4 or 5 rounds and you don't know which, design for round 4.**
   - Designing for round 4 loses at most about 4 votes per 10,000, whichever of the three rounds the vote falls on.
   - Designing for the final round 5 loses at most about 14 (if the game ends at round 3). Designing for round 3
     loses at most about 15 (if it ends at round 5).
   - All three are small next to designing for round 0 (up to 99). So "design for the final round" is a robust
     rule, and "design for the middle of the range" is slightly better still.
5. **Divided worlds are where timing matters.**
   - When the two biggest regions want very different things, the optimum moves about three times as far.
   - In those worlds a stale round-0 manifesto loses about 150 votes per 10,000 at round 5, against about 40 in
     united worlds.
6. **The library's ORACLE manifesto is the right election-day manifesto.** It is essentially b\*5: TV 0.004, and
   less than 1 vote per 10,000 worse.
7. **Zealots change little.** Without zealots the electorate drifts a bit more, so a stale manifesto costs slightly
   more (about 10 extra votes per 10,000 at round 5). The conclusions are the same.
